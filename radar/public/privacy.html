Privacy policy

What Radar keeps, and what you can take back.

Radar is used by students, some of whom are under 18. This policy is written to be read by them, not only by lawyers.

Last updated 23 September 2026

The short version

  • Radar stores what you type into it: your email, your profile answers, what you saved, and the notes on your applications.
  • It does not sell data, does not run advertising, and loads no third-party tracking scripts.
  • You can export everything or delete your account yourself, at any time, from account settings.
  • Counts of anonymous events are kept to see whether the product works. They cannot be traced back to you.

What is collected

Account

Your email address, your name if you give one, and a hashed form of your password. The password itself is never stored and cannot be recovered from what is stored.

Profile

The answers from onboarding: education level, year or grade, country and city, citizenship, subjects, goals, funding needs, experience, availability and preferred opportunity types. These exist to produce matches. You can change or clear any of them.

Your activity in Radar

Opportunities you save, applications you track, their stages, your private notes, checklist items, contacts you record and reminders you set.

Anonymous product counts

Radar counts events such as "a search happened" or "an application was submitted". Each row holds an event name, a coarse bucket and a timestamp. There is no user identifier, no session identifier, no IP address and no free text, so the counts cannot be joined back to a person. You can switch even these off in account settings.

Server logs

The web server keeps standard request logs for operating and securing the service. They are not used to build profiles.

Cookies and sign-in state

Running on its own server, Radar sets one cookie: a session token that keeps you signed in. It is HttpOnly, so page scripts cannot read it, and it expires after 30 days or when you sign out.

Running on Firebase, there is no Radar cookie at all. Firebase keeps your sign-in state in your browser's own storage instead, and it is cleared when you sign out.

Either way there are no advertising cookies and no third-party trackers, which is why there is no banner asking for consent Radar does not need.

Some preferences — a remembered filter, whether you muted the homepage sound — are stored in your browser's own storage and never sent to the server.

Students under 18

Radar is built for students, and many are minors. Because of that:

  • No more is asked for than matching needs. There is no date of birth field, no phone number, no address, no school name.
  • Profiles are private. Radar has no public profiles, no social features and no way for one user to see another.
  • If you are under 16, ask a parent, guardian or teacher before creating an account.
  • A parent or guardian can ask for a minor's account and data to be deleted through the contact form, and Radar will act on it.

How long things are kept

  • Account and profile: until you delete the account.
  • Saves, applications and reminders: until you delete them or the account.
  • Reports you file: kept while open; the contact email is removed once the report is closed.
  • Contact messages: kept up to 12 months so a conversation can be picked up again.
  • Anonymous counts: kept indefinitely because they contain nothing personal. On Firebase they are not collected at all.
  • Inactive accounts: after 24 months without a sign-in, Radar will contact you before deleting the account.

Your rights

You can, without asking anyone:

  • Export everything Radar holds about you as a JSON file, from account settings.
  • Correct any profile answer at any time.
  • Delete your account. Your profile, saves, applications, notes and reminders are removed immediately. The account row is kept only as an anonymised marker so nobody inherits your data by signing up with the same address.
  • Object to the anonymous counts by turning them off in settings.

Who else sees your data

No organiser, school, advertiser or data broker. Radar does not sell or share personal data with any of them. When you follow a link to an organiser's site you leave Radar, and that organisation's own privacy policy applies to whatever you do there — including any application you submit.

One company does hold data on Radar's behalf, and you should know who:

Google Firebase

Where this copy of Radar runs on Firebase, your account and everything under it — profile answers, saved opportunities, tracked applications, notes, checklists and reminders — are stored in Google's Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore. Google processes that data to run the service; it is not theirs to use for anything else, and Radar sends them nothing beyond what you typed into Radar.

Which storage a given copy of Radar uses is shown to you in account settings, under Storage.

Signing in with Google

If you choose Continue with Google, Google tells Radar three things about you: your email address, your display name and your account's unique identifier. Radar does not receive your Google password, and it asks for no access to Gmail, Drive, Contacts or anything else in your Google account.

Google will know that you signed in to Radar, in the same way it knows about any site you use Google to sign in to. If you would rather it did not, use the email and password option instead — the two accounts are identical in every other respect.

Deleting your Radar account deletes it from Firebase too, including the sign-in record. It does not touch your Google account, which is yours and is managed at myaccount.google.com.

Security

Passwords are hashed with scrypt and a per-account salt. Sessions are random tokens in HttpOnly cookies. The site sets a strict content security policy, and traffic is served over HTTPS in production. No system is perfect; if a breach affected your data you would be told what happened and what to do.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or how it is used, the date above changes and account holders are told before it takes effect.

Contact

Privacy questions and requests go through the contact form, marked "Privacy or data request".
