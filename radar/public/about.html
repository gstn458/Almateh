About

Built because the information gap is not a talent gap.

A student in Dubai and a student in Boston can be equally capable and hear about completely different things. Radar exists to close that distance.

The problem

Most opportunities are not secret. They are simply published somewhere a student was never going to look: a university department page, a foundation's PDF, a national committee's site that is updated twice a year. Whether you find them depends on who your school knows, and that is a poor way to distribute chances.

What Radar is

A catalogue of competitions, research programmes, scholarships and fellowships, each traced to the organisation that runs it. A matching engine that ranks them against a profile you control and explains itself. A tracker that carries an application from the day you find it to the day you hear back.

The standard every listing has to meet

  • It links to the organisation running it, not to another aggregator.
  • It states its own verification status honestly — including not yet verified.
  • It never asserts a deadline the organiser has not published.
  • It carries the date a person last read the source.

The catalogue is deliberately smaller than it could be. Adding a thousand scraped listings would be easy and would make Radar worse.

Where it is going

The next work is coverage: more of the Middle East, more non-STEM — arts, humanities, business, public service — and more opportunities that do not require leaving your own country to be worth having.

Who is behind it

Radar is built in Dubai. If you want to suggest an opportunity, correct a listing or ask something, the contact form reaches the person who maintains the catalogue.
