How Radar works

Four steps, and one promise about what Radar is not.

Radar is a directory, a matching engine and an application tracker. It is not the organiser of anything it lists, and it never decides whether you are eligible.

  1. Step one

    Build a profile

    Your level, subjects, goals, country, citizenship, funding needs, experience and what kinds of opportunity you want. It takes a few minutes and you can change every answer later.

  2. Step two

    Get matches you can argue with

    Nine signals produce every score: level, subjects, goals, location, funding, citizenship, deadline reachability, experience and preferred type. Each result shows the reasons it scored what it did, and the blockers that count against it — a citizenship rule you do not meet is stated, not buried.

  3. Step three

    Open the full listing, then the official source

    Every listing has an internal page with eligibility, funding, documents, application steps, the last date it was checked and a countdown. The organisation's own page is always one click away and is always the final authority.

  4. Step four

    Track it to a result

    Save it, add it to the tracker, work through eleven stages from researching to the outcome, keep private notes and a document checklist, set reminders, and export the deadline to your calendar.

What Radar does not do

It does not guarantee eligibility

A high match score means the facts you gave Radar line up with the facts on the listing. The organisation decides who may actually apply.

It does not control deadlines

Organisers move dates without telling anyone. Radar records when it last read the page, and you should check before you rely on a date.

It does not watch pages for you yet

Automatic deadline monitoring is not live. Verification is a scheduled human and link-check process, and the interface says which one applied.

It does not sell your data

No advertising, no data sale, no third-party trackers. See the privacy policy.
